Understanding Prostate Cancer Risk

Age, genetics, family history, and race all play a role in prostate cancer risk. Understanding your own risk is the first step toward early detection.

Age & Genetic Factors

Age and Family History Matter

Prostate cancer risk increases significantly after age 50. Approximately 60% of diagnoses occur in men over age 65.

Family history also plays a critical role. Men with a father or brother diagnosed with prostate cancer have double the risk compared to others.

Certain genetic mutations, including BRCA1 and BRCA2, may increase the likelihood of developing aggressive prostate cancer.

Race and Ethnicity

African American men and Caribbean men of African descent experience some of the highest prostate cancer rates in the world. Studies show they have nearly twice the risk of developing prostate cancer compared to other populations.

Prostate cancer is the second most common cancer among African American men, after skin cancer. Black men are up to 70% more likely to develop high-risk prostate cancer, and more than twice as likely to die of the disease, compared to white men.

Our initiative works to address these disparities through education, screening programs, and community outreach.

Prevention

Lifestyle & Prevention

Healthy lifestyle choices can help reduce risk. Early screening and healthy habits remain among the most effective tools for prostate cancer prevention and early detection.

  • Regular physical activity
  • Maintaining a healthy weight
  • Eating a diet rich in fruits and vegetables
  • Limiting processed and red meats
  • Avoiding tobacco use
  • Moderating alcohol consumption
